Milestone One

Through Chapter 10

First discussion — the setup

Key beats

  • Natalie wakes in a primitive 1855 cabin with no memory of how she got there; a group of children she doesn't recognize call her "Mama"
  • She's injured escaping through the woods (a steel trap mangles her ankle) and is nursed back by a stern girl, Mary, who insists this is her home and family
  • Flashbacks fill in her real life: a disciplined, isolating childhood; Harvard, where she clashes with roommate Reena; meeting Caleb Mills through a campus church group and marrying into his wealthy, politically connected family within months
  • A year after the wedding, a pregnant Natalie runs into a struggling Reena and gloats about her marriage, convinced she's won
  • Maeve, the gentler of the 1855 children, tells Natalie the name of the place: "Yesteryear" — the same name as the real-world ranch, a detail the reading club should hold onto

Quotes worth revisiting

"Yesteryear, Mama! That's what we call home."Maeve, Chapter 9, p. 105
"I waved goodbye, making a point to really wiggle my fingers so the diamond caught the light, and then I slipped past her, the first angry woman in my life, and skipped forward into the future."Chapter 10, p. 113

Big questions for discussion

  1. What's your working theory of what's actually happening in 1855 — time travel, a head injury, a hidden reality show, something else?
  2. How does the curated domesticity Natalie sells online compare to the real, exhausting chores she's now doing?
  3. What early red flags in the Caleb courtship read differently now that you know where this is headed?
Milestone Two

Through Chapter 30

Second discussion — the machine gets built

Key beats

  • Natalie's mother, Eliza, reveals her own coping trick — imagining an audience watching her — which Natalie adopts and eventually turns into her entire influencer career
  • Natalie and Caleb buy the Idaho ranch (naming it "Yesteryear Ranch") with $5 million from Caleb's father, Doug — funded on the explicit condition that Natalie give Caleb "a big American family"
  • The Instagram brand is built on almost nothing real: three years pass between six curated photos, while chicks and a dairy cow die of neglect and the crops fail behind the scenes
  • After two years of Caleb being unable to perform, Natalie resorts to self-insemination with a kitchen sauce baster to get pregnant again
  • In the 1855 timeline, house arrest tightens; Mary disappears into the woods for hours and comes back shaking, refusing to explain
  • Chapter 29 is the darkest point yet: Old Caleb moves back into Natalie's bed, and she reframes what happens as her "true role" and then as a religious test
  • Chapter 30 flashback: a pesticide scandal threatens the "organic" brand, and Natalie responds by disengaging from the farm entirely and refocusing on her own image

Quotes worth revisiting

"How much do you need?" "Five million dollars." […] "I want you to have more babies with my son."Doug, Chapter 24, pp. 194–195
"I don't trust anyone in this house, least of all myself."Chapter 23, p. 187
"This, right here, is my true role. To spread my legs when my husband demands. To have as many children as I can until my legs give out beneath me, and to feel nothing but gratitude on the hour of my death."Chapter 29, p. 242
"...the money was nearly gone."Chapter 28, p. 230

Big questions for discussion

  1. How does it reframe the "traditional family" once you know the ranch was a business deal from day one?
  2. What do you make of Mary's role — half caretaker, half warden — now that we've seen her come back from the woods shaking and unwilling to explain?
  3. Does the book's satire still land next to a chapter as dark as 29, or does the tone shift entirely?
Milestone Three

Through Chapter 45

Third discussion — cracks and witnesses

Key beats

  • Natalie takes a $1,500 social-media course from influencer Tammy Lane; her assigned mentor, Cassidy, tells her outright that she looks like she's pretending on camera — Natalie laughs it off ("Isn't everyone?") rather than absorb it
  • Mary has an unsettling, almost confessional moment with Natalie — "Has anyone ever lied to you?" — during which Natalie blurts out the names Shannon and Clementine before catching herself; Mary has never heard of Clementine at all
  • A men's-rights livestreamer praises Natalie on air as a "wholesome" traditional wife, and her followers jump from a few thousand to over 300,000 overnight — a viral moment she did nothing to earn
  • Abel turns thirteen and is taken by Old Caleb into the far woods for a private "initiation," leaving Noah behind, furious and inconsolable
  • In flashback, Natalie hires a new producer, Shannon, who starts out inspired by Natalie's "escape" from corporate life but grows visibly suspicious of the ranch's hidden staff and isolation — by Chapter 40 she's telling Natalie the ranch is "cursed"
  • Watching two bearded neighbor men working on a fence, Natalie has an unexplained, physical jolt of recognition looking at their eyes — and has to look away, nauseated
  • At a Chapter 45 dinner with those same neighbors, Natalie says out loud, to witnesses, that she's being held against her will — and no one reacts at all

Quotes worth revisiting

"You don't actually look happy. You look like you're grimacing."Cassidy, Chapter 32, p. 271
"Has anyone ever lied to you?"Mary, Chapter 33, p. 275
"Shannon," I manage. "Caleb. My father-in-law, bastard man, and Clementine—"Chapter 33, p. 277
"What's an ocean?"Clementine, Chapter 38 (paraphrased)

Big questions for discussion

  1. Why do you think no one reacts when Natalie says outright, at dinner, that she's being held against her will?
  2. What is Shannon starting to notice about the ranch that Natalie won't let herself see?
  3. What's your theory on the bearded neighbors — who are they, and why does Natalie react the way she does?
  4. Chapter 43 is the first time Natalie admits the people around her are real and matter to her, after 40+ chapters of imagining an audience instead. Is that a turning point, or just a new kind of performance?
Milestone Four

Through the End — Chapters 46–57, Part Three & Epilogue

Final discussion — the whole truth

Key beats

  • In flashback, Caleb falls for Shannon and tells Natalie he's leaving; the confrontation between the two women turns physical, with Natalie choking Shannon before catching herself — Shannon calls the ranch "cursed" and leaves
  • It turns out Clementine, not Shannon, has been quietly gathering evidence the whole time, using a phone Shannon gave her; Shannon's prime-time interview airs, reframing the assault for a national audience and turning Doug and Amelia against Natalie
  • Eliza finally confesses that she — not Natalie's father — was the one who cheated, decades ago, undercutting the one story Natalie's whole worldview was built on
  • In 1855, Maeve's illness pushes Natalie to finally ask Mary for real help; Mary reveals there was only ever one trap in the woods and helps Natalie find the path out — marked by triangles carved into birch trees
  • Natalie discovers a cabin marked "MANOSPHERE," and eventually a woman who looks exactly like her steps out of a Subaru: a grown Clementine, there to walk her through what actually happened
  • The full truth: the entire "1855" world was a hoax Natalie and Caleb built together to hide from prosecution after the Shannon assault; the "neighbors" are their own grown sons, Stetson and Samuel, who've been secretly keeping the family fed for years; Natalie is fifty and in menopause, not pregnant
  • The younger children are removed from the ranch; Natalie and Caleb, alone at last, agree they should have divorced years ago — and walk away from Yesteryear Ranch together anyway
  • Five years later, Natalie is serving a thirty-year sentence for child abuse, interviewed in prison by Reena about The Book of Mary — her daughter Mary's memoir, written from a new life in Santa Monica

Quotes worth revisiting

"I was squeezing her throat so hard that my knuckles had gone white. It was the recognition of this fact more than anything that caused me to let go."Chapter 48, p. 366
"a word is etched into the doorframe: MANOSPHERE. So this is where the men go."Chapter 53, p. 396
"'You're not pregnant, Mother,' Clementine says. 'You're going through menopause.'"Part Three, p. 432
"...the only future more terrifying to me than the one where we merged onto this highway was the one where we turned around."Epilogue, p. 453 — the novel's final line

Big questions for discussion

  1. Once you know "1855" was a deliberate hoax, do any earlier chapters read differently in hindsight? What clues do you wish you'd caught?
  2. Natalie spends the whole back half of the book suspicious of Shannon and completely misses Clementine. What does that blind spot say about her?
  3. Why give the family's final word to Mary, in her own memoir, rather than to Clementine — who did most of the actual rescuing?
  4. What do you make of the ending — Natalie and Caleb, having ruined each other and everyone around them, simply walking away hand in hand?
  5. Looking back at the whole novel: did the satire of tradwife/influencer culture hold up once the plot mechanics (the hoax, the abuse charges) took over — or did one undercut the other?
