Step 2 · 8 min

The story so far

A one-minute reminder of the sample, then the two parts of Batter’s life.

Who is Batter?A man in prison who tells his own story. He was named after a dog and loves poetry, but he has not written any.
What is Peck & Peck?A rare, expensive poetry quarterly with a quail egg on every cover. Batter first read it as a child, and it later closed.
Where are we?Batter has just graduated from college. He lies to his parents about a job in New York that does not exist.

Yuba City, California · 1967 and after

Childhood

  1. Chapter 2His mother takes sugar pills so his anxious father feels better.
  2. Chapter 3Career Week and the dodo: extinction comes from short-term thinking.
  3. Chapter 4His mother explains Helvetica, and why poetry “didn’t rate a serif.”
  4. Chapter 5Typefaces change feelings. His father says no one “goes into” poetry.

A New England university · 1975 and after

College

  1. Chapters 6–7His roommate Paul has a plan. Dr. Payne tells Batter to make a flight plan.
  2. Chapters 8–10Batter remembers Peck & Peck, changes majors, and finds poetry classes too much like math.
  3. Chapters 11–12The workshop goes badly, and he hears that Peck & Peck has closed.
  4. Chapters 13–14Poetry helps him understand the world. At graduation he invents a job.

Chapter 1 shows us the ending first: Batter is already in prison. How did he get there?

Step 2 · 8 min

The story so far

A one-minute reminder, then the long history of the Pecks and the week Issue 323 went to print.

The hidden historyBatter reads old articles about the Peck brothers and learns that twins have always run Peck & Peck. The Phone Book Parties are how it found new readers.
The rules of the gamePoems travel through three tiers of editors. Batter sees that the editors write their reports to please Salton, who knows little about poetry.
Crisis, lunch and troubleBatter stands up to Salton, learns that Declonius found every editor, and is called an editor. Then Mira starts causing him trouble.

The archives · 1900 to 1980

The Pecks and their parties

  1. Chapter 31Twin brothers Patrius and Argentio Peck, rich from Texas oil, start Peck & Peck in 1900. They want the best half percent of poetry and burn the rest.
  2. Chapter 31Four times a year they throw Phone Book Parties. The guests are chosen at random from the phone book.
  3. Chapter 31At a party in 1942, a young woman meets Wallace Stevens. He saves her from a flying baseball and points her to her future husband.
  4. Chapter 31In 1925 the next twins, Cusp and Rye, take over. During the Depression, submissions grow tenfold.
  5. Chapter 31Cusp’s twin sons, Declonius and Salton, are next. Declonius is a war hero who brings petrified trees home from France.
  6. Chapter 31Newspapers love Declonius. Salton, the money expert, almost disappears from the articles.
  7. Chapter 31Then the headlines change. Declonius is taken away in handcuffs for fraud and embezzlement.
  8. Chapter 31In 1980 Salton “rescues” the quarterly. The editors in the photo do not look safe.

Peck & Peck · publication week

Inside Issue 323

  1. Chapter 32Thirty-nine editors work in three tiers. Poems must arrive by mail, with a notarized letter, and certain topics go straight in the trash.
  2. Chapter 32Batter secretly copies great poems before they are burned. Poems are not allowed to leave the building.
  3. Chapter 33Two days before Issue 323, Batter finds a cocktail and a napkin invitation from Declonius in the copy room.
  4. Chapter 33At the Crisis Meeting, Batter speaks up about rogue waves. Salton keeps the rogue wave poem and Issue 323 is final.
  5. Chapter 34The editors take Batter for drinks and share how Declonius found each of them.
  6. Chapter 35At lunch Howard calls Batter an editor. A poet named Eduardo Jones asks Howard for help, and Howard promises to read his poem.
  7. Chapter 35Batter’s poem appears on city buses, in huge type. He lies to his parents again and says he is a “Copy Editor.”
  8. Chapter 36Mira makes trouble. Lou warns Batter that Mira will be Editor in Chief. Batter breaks the rule again, and someone catches him.

One twin is a hero who fell. The other runs everything and knows little. Which twin do you trust more?

The answer is Patrius Peck. He said it in the old articles Batter reads in Chapter 31.

2. “The bus, man. That was you, right?”

The answer is Eduardo Jones. He recognizes Batter’s name from the poem on the bus (Chapter 35).

3. “A rogue wave occurs in the middle of an ocean, not on a beach.”

The answer is Batter. He speaks up from the back of the Aviary in Chapter 33.

Round 2

4. “Love at first sight really did exist.”

The answer is Wallace Stevens. The librarian remembers his words at a Phone Book Party in 1942 (Chapter 31).

5. “Can’t take the beach out of the wave.”

The answer is Salton Peck. He says it in the Crisis Meeting in Chapter 33.

6. “The world of poetry publishing is cutthroat.”

The answer is Howard Gadley. He explains why no poems may leave the building (Chapter 32).
